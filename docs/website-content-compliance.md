## Purpose

The public website (kjslaw.com, `src/app/(site)` and `src/app/(site-es)`) is attorney advertising under California Rules of Professional Conduct 7.1–7.5. The old WordPress site published wrong phone numbers, a staff member's endorsement shown as a client testimonial, a case result with another case's details, and archived articles with superseded law and unsubstantiated superlatives. These rules keep them out; the tests enforce the checkable ones.

## Fees and costs: what a client pays without a recovery

- **The firm's decision (2026-09-24): the firm absorbs advanced costs when there is no recovery.** The client owes no fee and never repays the costs the firm advanced. A contingent-fee advertisement must say whether the client pays advanced costs when there is no recovery (Business and Professions Code § 6157.2); the site's statement is that disclosure.
- **One statement, one place.** `src/lib/marketing/no-recovery-terms.ts` (`noRecoveryTerms`): "No fees or costs unless there is a recovery." / "No cobramos honorarios ni costos a menos que haya una recuperación.", the same as a two-line card ("No fee or costs / Unless there is a recovery"), and the structured data's `priceRange`. The pages read it: the home hero and the "What to expect" panel (EN/ES), the closing strip of every practice, city, and why-hire-us page, the FAQ answers "What does the first consultation cost?" (personal injury) and "What does it cost?" (school liability, EN/ES), and the why-hire-us page (its fee reason, the last fee bullet, "What will it cost me?"). Change the terms there, and in the firm app's fee agreements (the retainer templates and the printed agreement live with the app), together.
- **Never "no financial risk", "risk-free", "sin riesgo".** A client who loses can be ordered to pay the other side's court costs (Code of Civil Procedure § 1032), and expert fees after refusing a § 998 offer.
- **Fee-only lines** ("No fee unless there is a recovery") need no cost disclosure while the client never repays costs (§ 6157.2). If the terms ever change, each needs the disclosure: the home trust rail and consultation paragraph (EN/ES), the why-hire-us hero list, meta description, and closing call (EN/ES), the four city pages' meta descriptions, the Spanish school-liability meta description, the web manifest, and the closing paragraph of the insurance-adjuster guide (`talk-to-insurance-adjuster-california`).
- **Archived posts** that promise the same in their own words ("you pay nothing until we win or settle your case", "no attorney fees or costs until I settle or win") are consistent with the decision: `july-4-car-accident-statistics`, `dog-bite-lawyer-on-strict-liability`, `looking-for-a-personal-injury-attorney-in-tustin`, `accident-victims-stacking-the-deck-against-you`, `lawyers-for-auto-accidents`, `tustin-car-accident-attorney-kyle-scott-wins-case-with-dash-cam`, `dog-bite-injuries-in-orange-county`. They would need rewriting if the terms change. (`when-should-you-hire-an-injury-attorney` says the free consultation carries no risk; it is about the consultation, not the case.)
- **The agreements say the same.** The firm app's retainer templates and printed fee agreement carry the same terms; they are maintained, and tested, with the app.
- **Tests.** `src/lib/marketing/__tests__/site-legal-statements.test.ts` fails on a copy of the statement written anywhere else in the site's code, and on a risk-free claim in any page or archived post.

## Archived articles (`src/lib/marketing/data/legacyPosts.json`)

- **The rule: the post's own text states the law.** When an archived post states the law in a way that is out of date or inaccurate, its own sentences are rewritten in place so they state the law correctly, reading as if they had been written that way: the post's voice and tone, its links and structure. Nothing on the page says the post changed: no editor's notes, no "updated" or "corrected" label, and no wording that it was wrong, incorrect, misstated, corrected, or fixed (the owner's instruction, 2026-09-24: such notes make the firm look bad). The revision date is metadata only: `editedOn`, and `modifiedTime`, which the structured data gives as `dateModified`. The page never displays it.
- **Notice.** Every archived post opens with the same neutral line, "Originally published <Month YYYY>. General information, not legal advice." (`legacyArchiveNoticeText` in `legacyPosts.ts`), and nothing about accuracy, changes, or corrections. The page's closing disclaimer and the sidebar's case-review link are unchanged. `needsLegalReview` (the import's flag for pre-2023 posts that discuss the law: 171 of 195) no longer changes the page.
- **Current law, as the site's guides state it.** A post that states one of these says it this way:
  - MICRA caps (Civil Code § 3333.2): "for 2026 the cap is $470,000 in an injury case and $650,000 in a wrongful death case, and it rises each January", matching the guide bullet "Medical malpractice caps." in `legalGuides.ts`. **Each January, update the guide and every post that states the caps together**; the test compares them.
  - Comparative fault: pure comparative fault since *Li v. Yellow Cab Co.* (1975). Fault reduces a recovery, and no percentage bars the claim (the guide: "at any percentage and does not bar the claim").
  - Minors: most personal-injury lawsuits are due two years after the child turns 18 (Code of Civil Procedure §§ 335.1, 352). A claim against a public entity must still be presented within six months of the injury, whatever the child's age (Government Code § 911.2; § 352(b)), and a late-claim application within one year (§§ 911.4, 911.6). See AGENTS.md hard rule 3.
  - Workplace harassment: three years from the last incident to file a complaint with the California Civil Rights Department (Government Code § 12960, since January 1, 2020). Other claims from the same conduct can have other deadlines.
  - Auto insurance: minimum liability limits of $30,000 / $60,000 / $15,000 (Vehicle Code § 16056, since January 1, 2025). A collision must be reported to the DMV within 10 days when anyone is injured or killed, or property damage is more than $1,000 (§ 16000).
  - Medical malpractice: attorney fees are limited to 25% of a recovery before a lawsuit or arbitration demand is filed and 33% after (Business and Professions Code § 6146, since January 1, 2023). A lawsuit is due one year from discovery or three years from the injury, whichever comes first (Code of Civil Procedure § 340.5).
  - Childhood sexual abuse: no time limit to sue for abuse on or after January 1, 2024 (Code of Civil Procedure § 340.1). Earlier abuse: until the survivor turns 40 or within five years of discovering the harm, whichever is later (§ 340.11). The 2020–2022 revival window has closed. No government claim is needed (Government Code § 905(m); § 340.11(s)).
  - Lawsuit deadlines: two years for most injuries (§ 335.1), but a claim against a public entity must first be presented within six months (Government Code § 911.2). State claims are presented to the Department of General Services (§ 911.2(b), since 2016).
  - Fault and premises: anyone whose carelessness caused the harm can be responsible, and the injured person's share only reduces the recovery. An owner's duty does not depend on whether the visitor was an invitee, licensee, or trespasser (*Rowland v. Christian*; Civil Code § 1714).
  - Dog bites: the owner is liable for a bite in a public place or to someone lawfully on private property, whatever the dog's history or the owner's care (Civil Code § 3342).
  - Underinsured motorist coverage pays for injuries, not vehicle repairs; repairs come from collision coverage or uninsured motorist property damage coverage of up to $3,500 (Insurance Code §§ 11580.2, 11580.26).
  - Punitive damages against a drunk driver: Civil Code § 3294 (*Taylor v. Superior Court*, 1979); the individual-defendant jury instruction is CACI No. 3940 (No. 3945 is for an entity).
